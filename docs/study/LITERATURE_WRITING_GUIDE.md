# Literature guide for the detection-methods outline

Reviewed 2026-09-21 at George's request. This is an editorial comparison of how five papers allocate explanation across methods, results and interpretation. It is not a new analysis, a literature-wide norm, or a template to copy. The current writing outline implements the recommendations; PAPER_ASSESSMENT.md remains the result/claim reference.

## Main finding

These papers do not all give detection statistics the same space. Each explains the parts of its method needed for its central scientific argument. Definitions, fitted freedom and the decision criterion are explicit; explanations become longer where a new estimator, inference procedure or model systematic is itself being studied. Results then use the quantities to explain physical trends, failures and observational implications.

For HWO-SLAPS, the organizing questions are mass reach across systems, the value of selection, the reliability of the Fisher approximation, and PSF effects. The nonlinear campaign supplies evidence for these questions. Its individual engineering checks do not determine the paper's structure. This is an editorial inference from the papers, not a rule stated by their authors.

## What each paper does

**Despali et al., “Detecting low-mass haloes with strong gravitational lensing I: the effect of data quality and lensing configuration” (2022).** Sections 2.1–2.2 introduce the model/posterior, smooth and perturbed evidences, and a position-dependent limiting mass. They explain marginalization over lens/source freedom and the adopted evidence criterion. The statistic is then used throughout Sections 4–6 to compare SNR, resolution, source structure, geometry and observing choices; sensitive area and best/mean limiting mass organize the comparisons. Appendices A–C develop macro-model readjustment, noise-realization and threshold tests. This is a good model for explaining what maps measure and why their spatial extent matters. Its evidence-based exclusion construction on smooth mock data is not our injection likelihood ratio.

Source: [local PDF](/Users/vassig/research/hwo/hwo-slaps/scratch/q1_observing_conditions/despali2022.pdf), arXiv 2111.08718v2, PDF pp. 2–3, 5–12, 14–17. The saved `writing_style_audit/despali.html` is the different 2018 paper and was not used as the 2022 source.

**He et al., “Testing strong lensing subhalo detection with a cosmological simulation” (2023; 2022 preprint/key).** Section 3.2 explains the successive model fits and their purposes, including local maxima and a spatial search. Section 3.2.4 then defines the maximum-log-likelihood difference and detection categories in a short operational explanation (<=5, 5–10, >10). Sections 4–5 use that comparison to examine smooth controls, injected subhaloes, parameter recovery and alternative mass models. Section 6 interprets model mismatch and applicability; Appendix A contains the parametric-source comparison. It is not a terse methods paper, but its detail serves the model-flexibility question. It provides a useful likelihood-based example without making its thresholds identical to ours.

Source: [saved published HTML](/Users/vassig/research/hwo/hwo-slaps/scratch/writing_style_audit/he.html), Sections 3–7 and Appendix A; [local PDF](/Users/vassig/research/papers/hwo/stronglensingsims.pdf). DOI 10.1093/mnras/stac2779.

**O'Riordan et al., “Sensitivity of strong lensing observations to dark matter substructure: a case study with Euclid” (2023).** Sections 3.1–3.3 give substantial detail on the classifier, its performance, and the conversion of mass/position trials into a sensitivity map. Equations 7–9 and Figures 4–5 explain probability-derived significance, log odds and the fitted mass dependence, including cases with no threshold crossing. Section 4 progresses from sensitivity distributions and area to population statistics and target selection; Section 5 discusses training/model limits, concentration and field haloes. The threshold-estimation procedure earns detail because it is part of the method's contribution. Our analogous explanation should define area and mass reach clearly, without borrowing classifier significance or their mass convention.

Source: [local PDF](/Users/vassig/research/papers/hwo/ORiordanEuclidForecasting.pdf), Sections 3–6, PDF pp. 5–14; arXiv 2211.15679, preprint dated 28 February 2023.

**Nightingale et al., “Scanning for dark matter subhaloes in Hubble Space Telescope imaging of 54 strong lenses.”** Section 3 is extensive: model families, source reconstruction, likelihood and fitting pipelines matter to the scientific test. The sampler itself receives a short Section 3.5; Sections 3.6–3.7 explain staged fitting, spatial scanning and the evidence difference, with candidate criterion Delta ln Z >10. The main Results investigate how lens light, source resolution and mass-model assumptions alter candidates; Discussion evaluates their credibility. Simulated-data validation is summarized in 4.1 and developed in Appendix B; regularization algebra is in Appendix A. The lesson is selective depth, not “put all technical content in appendices.” Our simpler fixed-template model does not require reproducing this source-inversion machinery.

Source: [saved published HTML](/Users/vassig/research/hwo/hwo-slaps/scratch/writing_style_audit/nightingale.html), Sections 3–6 and Appendices A–B; [local preprint PDF](/Users/vassig/research/papers/hwo/NightingalePipelineSimilarity.pdf), arXiv 2209.10566. Published HTML was preferred over the older PDF for section-specific claims.

**Wedig et al., “The Roman View of Strong Gravitational Lenses” (2025).** The main methodology explains survey/image simulation and lens-selection criteria. Discussion IV.1 uses a power-spectrum proxy for substructure versus instrumental effects. IV.2 introduces a separate idealized chi-square image-difference statistic, pixel selection, mass scan and significance criterion alongside the single-subhalo PSF experiment; IV.3 compares observing scenarios. Its statistical explanation is located where that experiment needs it, within a broader survey and instrument argument. This supports proportionate treatment of our PSF tests, not adopting its idealized detection statistic or treating it as nonlinear inference.

## Application to our outline

- **3.2:** explain the physical map, sensitive area and the complementary mass thresholds; then explain how masses are sampled/interpolated and how unreached thresholds enter ensemble comparisons. Keep the meanings and censoring in the main text. Statistical recipes and adaptive stopping do not each need a main-text paragraph.
- **3.3:** explain why nonlinear fits test the approximation, what can adjust, how likelihood maxima and detections are obtained, and the purposes/coverage of injection and control datasets. Summarize numerical reliability and point to its full specification. Do not organize the section around one upward-mass, tangent, bridge or recovery experiment.
- **Across the paper:** retain differences that affect interpretation, such as source freedom, search extent, PSF support and background treatment. Define them once and refer back when a comparison needs them. Put agreement/failure regimes in Results and observational/model limitations in Discussion; auxiliary tests may support those conclusions in an appendix.
- **Editorial discipline:** no paragraph/equation/figure quotas, compulsory vocabulary, or word-by-word imitation. A diagnostic deserves emphasis when it changes the scientific conclusion. A limitation deserves clear reporting, not automatic promotion into a new experiment or an organizing theme.
